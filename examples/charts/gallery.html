Examples · charts

Chart patterns for agent answers

Five forms that cover most questions a data agent answers, drawn from the real SpaceParts data (2022, euros, checked against labs/01-data-agent/verify_answers.sql). Each title states the finding; colour carries meaning, not decoration. Plain SVG and JavaScript, no libraries: copy the function you need.

Change over time vs plan · columns with a budget marker

Net sales by month, 2022, EUR. The line on each column is the budget; the label under it is the gap.

Use when the question is "how are we tracking against budget by month". Why: the budget is a target, so it's a marker, not a second bar or a second axis; the gap is written out so nobody has to estimate it. August is partial (data ends 21 Aug), so it's drawn lighter.

Gaps against budget · diverging bars, sorted

Key accounts, actual vs budget, Jan–Jul 2022, %.

Use when the answer is "who is behind". Why: one zero line, red below plan and blue above, sorted by the gap so the ranking is the answer. Direct labels at the bar ends; no legend needed.

Ranking · horizontal bars with the leader highlighted

Net sales by interplanetary region, 2022 to 21 Aug, EUR. Top 10 of 15; the rest are grouped.

Use when the answer is a ranked list. Why: horizontal bars leave room for long names; the one bar the title talks about gets the colour and the rest stay grey; a long tail becomes "Other" instead of slivers.

Same measure across groups · small multiples

On-time delivery, % of invoice lines, by month 2022. Six largest regions; same scale in every panel.

Use when one measure has to be compared across several groups over time. Why: one line per panel on a shared scale beats six tangled lines on one chart; the dashed reference is the overall average.

Plan vs plan · dot plot

Forecast vs budget by product type, full year 2022, EUR. Dot = forecast; tick = budget.

Use when two plans or two periods are compared item by item. Why: the connecting line is the gap; it reads faster than paired bars and keeps one axis.

What to avoid

Patterns that make answers harder to trust

  • Two y-axes. Use two charts or small multiples.
  • Smoothed lines. They invent values between months.
  • Budget as a second bar or line in a bold colour. It's a target: draw a marker.
  • Pie charts for more than three parts. Use sorted bars.
  • A label on every point. Label the ends and the point the title is about.
  • Totals inside a chart of their parts. The total flattens everything else.
  • Rainbow categories. Grey for context, colour for the point.
  • Neutral titles ("Sales by month"). Say the finding.
